Solution register model, version 2.34

Two Ways Into the Registers

The same six item types, followed from the two places work actually starts on a vendor build: a stakeholder stating a need, and a discovery that the solution falls short. Open items are the only thing anyone works. Everything else is a record that an open item changes.

REQWhat we need. Owned by whoever said so.
LIMWhat the solution cannot do. A fact.
DECA choice made, or a shortfall accepted.
RSKAn uncertainty with a trigger: a risk, an assumption or a dependency.
CRA change to agreed scope, with a cost.
OIWork. The bridge between records.

Requirements first

Trigger: a stakeholder or SME states a need. The requirement is a record from the moment it is written, and it carries no next action of its own. Each move along its track is made by an open item, and the Designed state is the only place the register points into a design document.

Limitations first

Trigger: while designing or building, we or the vendor find that the solution will not do, or does differently, something we need. The row is a fact. The assessment is an open item, and its first job is to find the requirement the limitation constrains. Only then are options sketched and one chosen, and the chosen option picks exactly one exit.

The change proposal's own track

A proposal tracks work emerging through iterative requirements and design development. Source links to the solution design; an optional based on link references the option-selection Decision. Vendor ref identifies an externally managed vendor change request, when one exists. Scope and Phase identify the delivery commitment. Estimates should state Indicative or Confirmed, with their source and date; blank means Not sized.

When the exit is Change requested, the CR carries the rest of the change's life on one row. The limitation is closed the moment the CR exists. Whether to change was settled on the limitation. The CR is what the business approves: the justification in their terms, the estimate they are approving, and who approved it.

Where the designs sit

Option sketches

Made during LIM assessment so that each option can carry an impact. A paragraph or a diagram per option, on a page linked from the limitation. Disposable once the option is chosen. The vendor's estimate is an input here.

Build design

Made only for the chosen option. For a change, on a page named in the CR's Source while it is Proposed, then folded into the design document section at Delivered. For a workaround, the procedure in an operations document, pointed at by the DEC's Source.

The register never holds a design. It holds a Source pointer, and a requirement's Designed state says a design exists. That is what lets design documents be split per service or combined without changing tracking.

Risks, assumptions and dependencies

One register, one lifecycle, three kinds. A programme manager can list the dependencies and the assumptions on their own, and nothing new needs working: the verifying and the chasing are mitigation open items, like any other work.

KindTriggerMitigationRealised meansRetired means
Riskthe event that says it has happenedwhat is being doneit happened, open item raisedno longer possible or worth tracking
Assumptionevidence that it is falsehow and when it will be verifiedit was false, open item raisedverified true, or replaced by a decision

Likelihood and Impact are the chance and the cost of the assumption being wrong or the dependency slipping. The party and the needed-by date go in the Trigger text. A dependency on the vendor can be owned by "Vendor: name".

Where this console fits

An engagement runs in one of two write modes. In the default, direct, what you see is the registers as they stand: each move, edit or new item rewrites the item file, stamps its Updated date and adds a line to its History section naming you, the move, the reason and the evidence; git holds the rest. In change-sets mode what you see is the registers as proposed: the item files plus every change set not yet applied. Each change appends one block to your session's change set, a dated file, and the ingester applies it through its gate, settles any conflict, and assigns ids. The mode is a line in the engagement file, and the banner at the top says which is in force.

One table, a side panel, a bulk bar

Once an engagement is Live, everything runs over one table of items: the work queues, the registers and a rule's failing rows are the same table with a different filter and a different editable column, not separate pages. A row's fields edit in place; opening it puts the whole item in a side panel over the table, with what it came from and what it produces either side of its next moves. Selecting several rows raises a bulk bar for the change common to all of them. Whichever way a state change is asked for, cell, panel or bulk bar, the same form opens and asks only for the fields and links that move needs, sharing what several selected items agree on and listing what each one still lacks. A form with nothing missing is the only one that can be applied.

Starting from an existing register: the baseline

Some engagements begin with generated or handwritten registers in Confluence. Pull those pages as source evidence, choose which contain register rows, then select Import and review. The console creates working register items with source references and opens a review batch. There is no separate freeze workspace.

Edit actual items directly in Rationalise, singly or in bulk. Type changes and merges show previews before acceptance. Saved reviews remain available for older batches and optional splitting. A correction can establish the known current position directly without creating historical open items. Current work continues to follow the lifecycle above.

The source pages and historical baseline/frozen.md ID map remain available for provenance and push compatibility. Initial import refuses to overwrite existing registers. You can start another review over any existing records whenever needed.

The edge case, day by day

The same story as a sequence of register changes, using option b as the chosen path.

Day 0

Vendor design review surfaces an edge case that REQ-0014 does not cover cleanly. LIM-0021 created, Identified, Impact one line, Source = review date, Owner = who found it. OI-0045 raised to assess, worker named.

Day 2

OI-0045 confirms with the requirement owner that the edge case matters. Links on the limitation gain constrains REQ-0014. LIM moves to Under assessment.

Day 5

Three options sketched on the options page. Vendor estimate obtained for a. Options and impacts written on the row: a) vendor change, cost and 6 weeks, this phase; b) manual process, 2 hours a week ops, this phase; c) nothing, 3 customers a month affected.

Day 8

Stakeholder forum picks b. Chosen option = 2. DEC-0011 created and Accepted: Rationale names b and the options it beat, Consulted lists vendor and ops SME, Approved by the forum. LIM moves to Accepted with Links dispositioned by DEC-0011. OI-0045 closes with resolves into DEC-0011.

Day 8

Ops needs a weekly report to find the affected customers. REQ-0031 raised in Draft, Implemented by = Internal, owner the ops lead. Residual volume is uncertain, so RSK-0003 raised by DEC-0011: Trigger = more than 20 cases in a week, review monthly.

Day 15

Procedure written in the ops document. DEC-0011 Source points at it. REQ-0014 moves on to Delivered when the vendor build lands, shortfall known and traceable.

The one rule that keeps this simple. A stakeholder makes one call per limitation, and it is recorded in one place. For a change, on the CR's approval. For an acceptance, on a DEC. Never both.
